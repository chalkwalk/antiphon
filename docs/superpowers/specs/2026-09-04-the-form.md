# The form: phrases that return

Design, 2026-09-04. Written before any of it is built.

**What this is for.** The band plays one groove for as long as it plays. There
is no structure above the interval, so a long session is a single texture --
`ROADMAP.md`, *Phrases that return*.

**Everything it depends on has landed.** The seed split named the three holds;
the foundation stratum gave the kit and the bass one figure to share;
`performanceSeed` made two intervals of the same figure differ. That last one is
the precondition: this is the first change that can put two identical intervals
side by side, and it is only safe because their performances now differ.

---

## 1. What a section is, and what it changes

A **section** is a run of intervals sharing one letter of a form. The form is a
table -- `AABA`, `ABAC`, `AAAB` -- chosen from the room seed, so `shake` reaches
the shape of the music and not only its notes.

**A section changes the whole groove: the kick's figure, the bass's figure and
the lead's contour.** That is the strongest form signal available and it is a
deliberate choice over two smaller ones, because a form nobody can hear is
decoration.

**It changes no harmony at all.** The chart and the key are one chart and one
key for the whole session -- `ROADMAP.md`'s hard constraint, and the reason is
the interval delay: you hear the band a whole interval late, so the form you
hear is rotated against the form they are playing. A rotation of the same chords
is the same chords. Different chords per section would have you soloing over a
progression you cannot hear.

### 1.1 What stays session-held, and why

- **The phrase length.** Built two days ago and deliberately not sectioned: a
  section that changed both its figure and how often the figure comes round is
  two changes wearing one name, and the second would be inaudible under the
  first.
- **Patches, instruments, the kit's snare and hat patterns.** These are what
  makes the band sound like itself. A B section on different instruments is a
  different band.
- **The tempo, the metre, the key, the chart.** Not the band's to change.

---

## 2. B is a transformation of A, not an independent roll

The risk of sectioning the whole groove is that B stops sounding like the same
tune and the room has to re-learn it every few intervals. So B is **A's figure
with its pulse count changed**, not a fresh roll: the same rhythmic idea at a
different density.

**It is density rather than rotation, and that is forced.** Both foundation
figures pin `rotation = 0` on purpose -- *"the kick lands on the downbeat;
everything else moves"* (`BotBand.cpp:70`). Rotating either for a B section
would displace the downbeat, which is a larger and worse change than a section
is meant to make, and would undo an argument the code already won.

**A DIFFERENT density, not necessarily a greater one.** The direction comes
from the seed per letter, so some sections lift and some thin. Two constraints
follow and both are handled rather than hoped:

- **The range is bounded at both ends**, so at the extremes "different" can only
  go one way. The kick's own range starts at three pulses -- sparse enough to
  leave room, dense enough to be a groove -- and no figure may exceed its steps.
  A delta that clamps back onto A's count is reflected rather than accepted.
- **A form may have more than one non-A letter.** `ABAC` needs B and C to differ
  from A *and* from each other, so the delta is a function of the LETTER rather
  than a coin flipped per section.

The bass's pulse count is nudged coprime with its phrase, so a transformed
count is re-nudged at the same scale. That rule was never about the specific
number; it is about a figure not sub-repeating inside its own phrase.

---

## 3. How long a section is

**From a duration, not a count.** The seed picks a target of roughly twenty to
forty seconds; the metre converts it to a number of intervals:

```
intervalSeconds  = bpi * 60 / bpm
intervalsPerSection = clamp(round(target / intervalSeconds), 2, 8)
```

So a section feels like a section whatever the room's tempo and metre, and the
seed still reaches the form's proportions, which is what `ROADMAP.md` asks of
it.

**Never one interval, and that bound is not taste.** You hear the band an
interval late. If a section is one interval long, the boundary you hear is a
whole section out of step with the one you are playing over -- the form is
maximally misaligned. At N intervals the misalignment is 1/N of a section, so it
lands inside the right section rather than the previous one.

**The upper bound is attention.** Eight intervals at bpi 32 is over two minutes
of one letter, which stops being a section and becomes the tune.

---

## 4. The form origin, and everything that resets it

The form is read from an **origin**, not from the absolute interval index, so
that it can restart.

| trigger | why |
|---|---|
| `BandPlayState` Silent -> Playing | a band coming in mid-structure is not what "start playing" means |
| a key change | a figure returning over harmony that moved is not a return |
| a chart change | the same |
| **a tempo or metre change** | **new: the section LENGTH is derived from them, so a change moves the boundary under a section already in progress** |

The last one is new with this design and is a consequence of section 3: making
the length a duration makes the tempo part of the form. A `!vote bpm` that
carries mid-tune would otherwise leave the band half way through a section of a
length that no longer exists.

`ROADMAP.md` already carries the first three under *Starting a tune starts the
form*; the fourth joins them, and all four are one mechanism with four triggers.

**One interval of overlap is inherent and no reset fixes it.** A change during
interval N is one the room plays over while still hearing the band's interval
N-1. That is the interval delay rather than the form, it lasts exactly one
interval, and it is true today with no form at all.

---

## 5. The turnaround marks the boundary

**A form nobody can locate is not a form.** A listener an interval behind cannot
infer where a section begins from the notes alone, which is what promotes the
turnaround from decoration to the thing that makes the rest audible.

The drums already fill the last interval of every four (`intervalIndex % 4 ==
3`). That becomes **the last interval of every section** instead -- the same
gesture, aimed at the structure rather than at a fixed count. It is why the
ending has a first interval at all: the fill is what tells the room the next
downbeat is a boundary.

**It will usually NOT be every fourth interval any more**, and that is worth
saying plainly rather than implying continuity. At a thirty-second target the
count runs 2 to 8 across ordinary rooms -- three intervals at bpi 16 and
100 bpm, four at 120, two at bpi 32, the clamp at bpi 8. Four was a fixed guess
at "the phrase length a listener hears whether or not anyone intended one"; it
becomes the actual phrase length, which is the whole point of the item.

---

## 6. What this does not do

- **No per-section intensity.** *A shared intensity curve* is its own roadmap
  item and a different axis: intensity varies within a section, the form varies
  between them.
- **No deviation.** An occasional departure whose likelihood grows with the
  repeat count belongs to the figure seed and the repeat count, and wants a form
  to depart from -- which it now has, so it is next rather than never.
- **No `band, play ABACBA`.** Being told a form is cheap once the mechanism
  exists and is deliberately last: the default form has to be good before
  choosing one is interesting.
- **No change to the pad, lead or accent strata.** They arrive with *A band of
  more than four*.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Denser or sparser for B**, per section 2. Wants ears, and cannot get them
   until the form renders.
2. ~~**Whether a held contour across a LONG section starts to sound
   looped.**~~ **ANSWERED by measurement, 2026-09-04: it does not.** Over 60
   seeds in D minor at bpi 16, comparing consecutive intervals that happen to
   draw the same contour -- which is what a section would make happen every
   time -- the lines share a mean of **43.9%** of their notes, and **none of 211
   pairs was effectively identical**. Consecutive intervals with DIFFERENT
   contours share 14.1%, so the contour is doing real work; `carryIn` and the
   chart are doing enough underneath it that holding it cannot produce a loop.
   A returning A is the same idea, differently said.
3. ~~**Whether the target duration should be seed-varied**~~ -- **yes**, and
   the roadmap asks for it: `shake` should reach the shape of the music. One
   caveat worth writing down rather than discovering: the seed's reach shrinks
   as the metre grows. At bpi 32 an interval is about 19 seconds, so every
   target in a 20-40 second range clamps to two intervals and the seed changes
   nothing. The variation is real at bpi 8 and 16 and nominal above it.

---

## 8. Order of work

1. The form itself: the table, the section length, the origin and its four
   resets. Pure functions, testable without audio.
2. Sectioning the three decisions, and B as a transformation. Audible; the
   parity diff is expected to be non-empty and is reviewed by listening.
3. The turnaround follows the section boundary.

*Deviation* follows in `ROADMAP.md`'s order and is not part of this.
